# Changelog

Every change that reached `main`, newest first, in plain words. The version in `VERSION` is the newest heading here; `.github/scripts/check_version.py` checks the two agree on every pull request.

How the numbers work, big.step.bugfix:
- big: 0 until the CAD is good enough for real parts; 1.0.0 is that release.
- step: the CAD roadmap step being built (`docs/progress/cad.md`), so 0.24 is step 24, the timeline. A step's number goes up when its first piece merges, and the bug-fix number starts again at 0. (In the first chat each step was finished in one go, so its number went up when it was done; step 15's start sits under 0.14.1.)
- bugfix: goes up by one for each merged pull request whose main job is fixing something broken (a bug, a crash, a failing or flaky check).
- Everything else (integration, AI, guide, testing and CI work) doesn't change the number; it is listed under the version `main` had when it merged.

Versions 0.0.0 to 0.14.2 come from the first chat's own commits, which reached `main` in four big early pull requests (#1, #2, #7, #8). From 0.15.0 on, numbers like #154 are the pull requests.

## 0.32.16 (2026-10-26)

- Docs: the shared rule files say what #336 changed, so a thread starting random testing on its branch knows the rules: root `CLAUDE.md` lists `stop-testing.yml` (cancels a branch's runs when its pull request merges or closes); `kernel/CLAUDE.md` says branches' dispatched runs go one at a time and give way to waiting CI (two minutes, a newer run of the same branch, an hour for another branch's), and that `ts_mutants` is the tick for the TypeScript packages' mutation run. No code changes.
- Fixed: an edge crossing a face right on one of the face's edges, where that edge met the crossing edge only at a corner of the crossing edge, was placed on the wrong spot (at the corner rather than where the two edges are nearest), and when two face edges were both within reach the one picked depended on where the face's loop started. The holding face edge is now read at the two edges' nearest points within reach, and the nearest face edge wins, ties broken by position, never by loop order. Regression tests failed first, among them issue #352's own case (frontier testing's same-answer check: the generator's side touch at 1 km, B moved 2.2e-5 mm, on the nearer edge from every loop start).

- Fixed: two long edges crossing at an angle smaller than the angle tolerance (a few billionths of a radian) were treated as parallel, so their crossing was lost. The closest-points routine now only falls back to the parallel case when the two are exactly parallel. Regression test (and the seeded closest-points test, now with every eighth pair leaning by 1e-9 to 1e-13 rad) failed first.

- Fixed: an edge lying along an edge of a face (an overlap) could also be given a crossing of its own inside that overlap, two facts for one place. A crossing inside an overlap is now left to the overlap. Regression test failed first.

- Fixed: the "is this point inside the face" test walked straight from corner to corner, but a valid corner may sit up to its tolerance off its edge's curve, so a point clear of the real edge could be called outside (and an edge crossing the face there was lost), or inside when it was outside. The test now walks each edge along its curve, from the corner to the curve's start, along the curve, then to the next corner. Four hand-worked regression tests failed first.

- Fixed: two corners of a face both within tolerance of one place along an edge lying in that face were each given their own mark, two points for one place. That is now the clean rule-1 refusal naming both corners. Regression test (0.9× and 1.1× of where the two corners' reaches meet) failed first.

- Fixed: an edge lying in a face, whose short stretch next to one of the face's corners is within reach of both edges meeting there, had that stretch labelled "along" whichever edge the face's loop listed first (issue #355, frontier testing's same-answer check: seed 30008's prisms touching along an edge at 100 km, moved 2.2e-5 mm, changed answer when the loops started one coedge later). The stretch now lies along the nearer edge at its middle. For crossings and stretches alike, edges as near as each other to rounding (a few ulps, which moving or turning the part changes) are now taken by position, not by which rounded distance came out smaller: the exact model found one such tie (seed 30003, boxes touching along an edge, a crossing exactly as far from two edges). Hand-worked test of the tie rule at 0.9× and 1.1× of the rounding allowance; both real cases failed first. #328's exact model now applies the same nearest-edge rule and agrees on five seeds.

## 0.32.15 (2026-10-26)

- Testing: a hand-worked lib test for `tessellate.rs`'s `reaches_into` with a chord that shares one end with an arc's chord and reaches into the arc with its other end (and one whose other end is outside), from a 101-mutant sample run by the 10-26 hunt: deleting the `!` before `shared[0]` survived (the nightly whole-kernel run that would have caught it has not finished since 10-23, fixed in 0.32.8). The test fails against that mutant and its `shared[1]` twin, both tried by hand.
- Fixed: six more kernel bugs from the cold code-reading hunt (six helpers read different areas, a seventh checked every finding), each with a test that failed first. (1) A sketch of two arcs placed on a grid so that they touch at a corner and cross again a hair past it (the arcs' centres at (0.001, 1) and (0.002, 2.001), a shape a user can draw) passed the sketch checks and then failed to extrude with a kernel error. The rule is now that two pieces which meet again anywhere past their shared corner cross, however close that second meeting is, so such a sketch is refused up front with the two pieces named; the old allowance for a second meeting within the tolerance is gone (a sliver that thin is below what the maths can tell apart far from the origin, so building it would hide the problem, not solve it). No tolerance was loosened. (2) An edge tilted a little out of a circle's plane could graze that circle within tolerance millimetres from where it crosses the plane, and the self-intersection check never measured it (217 of 300 random grazing pairs missed); the closest approach is now a candidate too. Only the arc of the circle the edge can reach is searched (a circle point is at least its distance from the plane crossing times the tilt from the line, and no entity's tolerance is looser than 500 nm), so a rim far from an edge costs nothing: the first version searched every rim against every nearby edge and made validating a 24-corner star 29% slower, now 6% (CI's speed baseline, limit 15%). Two circles tilted against each other are still only checked where they cross each other's planes; that needs its own design and is handed to the boolean work (step 43). (3) A ball with one hemisphere flipped inside out (two coincident sheets enclosing nothing) passed validation about half the time, whenever the rounding of its volume came out positive; a shell enclosing no more than the rounding of its own size is now refused. (4) An edge used twice by the same face (a slit) passed every check; it is refused. (5) Turning a body many times about a far pivot walked it outward (10,000 turns of 0.001 rad about a pivot 10 m away pushed it 12 µm out, past the tolerance): the turn now keeps the body's distance from the axis exactly (100,000 turns stay within 6 µm). (6) An arc of radius exactly 1 km, the limit, could be refused for a rounding of 1e-10 mm; the limits allow their own rounding. (7) Found by the new tests: the kernel's zero finder (used wherever curves meet surfaces and each other) could lose a zero sitting exactly on the shared end of two search pieces, because the midpoint's rounding (2e-16 either way) outweighed the margin it relied on below piece widths of about 2e-5 rad; 1,869 of 3,000 staged widths lost the zero. It now measures from the midpoint to each end as they really are and allows for the evaluation's own rounding. Verified by the whole kernel suite (596 unit tests, devious parts, stateful harness, scale sweep, fuzz corpora), clippy, the WebAssembly build, mutation testing of the changed lines (the surviving rounding ties excluded with reasons), the speed baseline measured locally, and random testing on the branch.

## 0.32.13 (2026-10-26)

- Fixed: the boolean's facts table crashed on ordinary parts. When an edge of the tool lay inside a longer edge of the target (a box sitting flush on a longer box, say), the check for "is this tool corner the same place as a target corner already listed" looked the tool corner's number up in the target's list. That read the wrong corner, and when the tool had more corners than the target it ran off the end of the list and the kernel panicked. Found by a cold read of step 32's code. Two regression tests failed with the panic first (one edge inside another, worked by hand; a 3 × 1 × 1 box with a cube sitting flush on its top as part of a 16-corner tool) and pass now.

- Fixed: a valid part with loose (imported-size) tolerances could be refused by the facts table even where the other part touched nothing of it. A corner may sit as far as its own tolerance plus an edge's from its own face's plane and still be valid, but the table measured every corner against its own faces and, past the cap, refused: a cube at 500 nm with one corner 800 nm off its own top was refused with the other part 5 mm away. A corner nothing touches is now kept as it is, with its own tolerance; one the other part does touch is still measured (the same corner with the tool's corner on it is still refused, rightly, because a cut line in that face would end there). Regression test failed first.

- Fixed: the facts table could miss where two edges cross. When a corner of one part touched an edge of the other only through its own looser tolerance (a corner grown to 100 nm by an earlier boolean, its edges still 10 nm), the table took it to mean the two edges meet only at that corner, even when they really crossed 10 µm further along; with the crossing just outside the part, no point was made there at all, which the next step would have tripped over. A corner now holds the meeting only when it is itself within the two edges' own tolerance of the other edge; otherwise the crossing is recorded as well. Regression test failed first (at 0 and 5 nm, and 0.9× and 1.1× of the edges' tolerance); step 32a6b's exact-model check of every generator pair still agrees.

- Fixed: one place along an edge lying in a face could be marked twice, leaving a stretch of no length that was then called inside or outside right on the face's boundary. Two ways: the edge-crossing fix above asked only the target's corner whether it held a meeting, not the tool's corner that is the same place; and a corner or edge of the face could come within tolerance of the edge's curve at its very end while the corner at that end (which may sit up to its tolerance off the curve's end) did not. The first now asks both corners. The second is now the clean rule-1 refusal naming both (they are neither one place nor clearly apart), and when the end's corner does touch it, the end's own mark holds it. Found by the second cold hunt's seeded in-plane edges (3,000 edges per tolerance mix against an L-shaped face with a hole, up to 140 failures per mix); four hand-worked regression tests (0.9× and 1.1× of the summed tolerance) and the seeded test, now permanent, failed first and pass now.

- Fixed: a valid input with an edge looser than its own corners is now refused up front (heal it to tighter tolerances), instead of reaching facts that assume corners are at least as loose as their edges. Regression test failed first.

- Fixed: an edge of one part crossing a face of the other within tolerance of two corners of that face at once would have made those two corners one point. Now the rule-1 refusal naming both corners; where several corners could hold a crossing, the nearest is chosen, never the first in memory. Regression tests (including 0.9× and 1.1× of where the two corners' stretches meet) failed first.

- Also: the kernel's WebAssembly build no longer warns about an unused name in the merge's failure report.
