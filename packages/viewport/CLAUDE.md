# Viewport

Contents of `packages/viewport`; the rules are in packages/CLAUDE.md and the root CLAUDE.md. Keep this list current when you add or change a file here.

- the one Three.js viewport every workspace draws into (`Viewer`, implements `SharedViewport`: one scene seen through one or more `ViewportView`s, each its own camera, for split view; `paneViewports` gives each workspace on screen a `SharedViewport` over its own view), the camera re-framing rule, and the image sheet with its top-down 2D mode (`image-sheet.ts`, G1b; each view shows its own image on a layer only its camera sees; `showImage(null)` is the same 2D view with no sheet, for a workspace with nothing open).
- the backdrop (`backdrop.ts`, UX2): a near-black blue gradient, lighter at the top, painted by `paintBackdrop` before each frame's scene as 32 scissored clears of flat colour (the renderer's `autoClearColor` off), since a textured background slowed workspace switching by half in the browser checks' software renderer; `backdropAt(fy)` gives its colour a fraction down the canvas (`test/backdrop.test.ts`; the e2e pixel checks use `apps/fission/e2e/backdrop.mjs`).
- sketches (step 21c): `showSketches` draws sketch lines over the body (the edited one with its grid, points and rubber band; the ground grid hides meanwhile), `showPlanes` the origin planes to pick (`ViewportPick.plane`), `onSketchPointer` gets clicks and hovers on the edited sketch's plane instead of picking; the maths (plane frame, ray meets plane, grid lines, `pixelSize`: mm per screen pixel at the pointer, sent as `SketchPointer.pixel` for snapping) in `sketch-view.ts`; a drawing's `editing.snap` marks are drawn in green over everything, its label in `data-sketch-snap`.
- picking where a sketch goes (step 26d): `showPlanes(planes, faces)` also offers body faces; while planes are shown a click gives the face under the pointer whenever there is one, else the nearest offered plane (`planeOrFace` in `picking.ts`), never an edge, and only an offered plane (brighter, `PLANE_OPACITY`, `data-hover-plane`) or offered face lights; the planes are drawn behind the body (opaque pass with their own blending, `PLANE_ORDER`), so what shows is what a click picks; `data-pick-faces`. `lookAt(plane, side)` and `SketchDrawing.side`: from side −1 (a back or bottom face) the camera is on the other side, up y or −y as the nearest standard view has it (`standardUp` in `views.ts`), so a bottom face is seen as the bottom view sees it.
- seeing what you draw (sketch feel SF2): while a sketch is edited the body is see-through (`BODY_FADE`, `data-body-faded`), the model axes give way to the sketch's red x and green y axes, and the grid follows the main view's camera (`sketchGrid`, `gridSpacing`, `minorStrength` in `sketch-view.ts`: powers of ten at least 8 px apart, or the snap grid's multiples when `editing.gridSnap`; redrawn in `Viewer.frame` when the camera moves; `data-sketch-grid`, `data-sketch-axes`); `lookAt(plane)` puts a view's camera square onto a plane (`planeViewBasis` in `views.ts`, framing from `lookAtFraming` in `framing.ts`).
- moving the camera by hand (sketch feel SF3a, the owner's choice of bindings): `navigation.ts` holds the maths (`zoomAt`: camera and target scale about the focus-plane point under the cursor; `panBy`: the grabbed point follows the pointer, `focusPixel` mm a pixel; `orbitBy`: Z-up spherical turn, kept `POLE_EPS` off the poles; `wheelFactor`, `dragZoomFactor`, `clampZoom`, `cubeDragAngles`; `dragAction`: middle pans, Shift+middle orbits, the right button is kept for a menu, left follows the `NavMode`); `ViewportView.navigate()` wires the wheel (Ctrl = trackpad pinch), middle-drag and Pan/Zoom-mode left-drag ahead of OrbitControls (capture), which keeps orbiting and touch; dragging the view cube orbits (`viewcube.ts`). `nav-bar.ts`: each view's navigation bar (`.view-controls`, styles in packages/ui `styles/view-controls.css`): Orbit, Pan, Zoom (`data-nav`, `aria-pressed`; the view's `data-nav`), Fit, Look at (on only while a sketch is edited), Home, Top, Front, Right (`data-view`). `cameraPose()` is read by the e2e test through `fissionTest.camera`.
- the guide (How SpindleLabs works; opened by the app's Guide button or a `?guide` link): `guide/chapters.ts` the words (23 chapters and the word list; each special word explained where it is first used, checked by test), scroll maths (`chapterAt`, `chapterForKey`, `cameraDistance`, `stageLayout`); `guide/bracket.ts` the B-rep example bracket written out face by face (Euler–Poincaré 16 − 24 + 10 − 2 = 0); `guide/scenes.ts` one scroll-driven Three.js scene per chapter (`update(p)`, labels as `anchors`, the maths each shows as small exported functions with hand-worked tests, `aimCamera` sets y-up since the app's default is z-up); `guide/guide.ts` the full-screen page (`openGuide`: sticky cards over the scene, labels with leader lines, rail and Next, keys and clipboard kept inside, a Licences link beside Close opening the app's `third-party-licences.txt` (audit L2); `data-chapter`, `data-progress`, `data-frames`, `data-webgl` for the e2e test). Styles: packages/ui `styles/guide.css`.
- Extrude in the view (UX1b): `extrude-pick.ts` the maths (`insideOutline`/`insideLoop`: the even-odd rule exact for arcs, the chord polygon XOR each arc's segment; `loopPoints`/`arcPieces` the outline drawn within `FILL_SAG` of its arcs; `arrowDistance`: the arrow's line nearest the pointer's ray, none along the arrow, `ALONG_ARROW`; `snapDistance` whole mm, never 0; `arrowLength`, `ARROW_PX` on screen, `ARROW_GRAB_PX`), `extrude-layer.ts` (`ExtrudeLayer`: the outlines' see-through fills, brighter hovered and chosen, `FILL_OPACITY`; the preview, `PREVIEW_OPACITY`; the arrow over everything), `viewer.ts` `showExtrude`/`onExtrudePointer` (a click picks the nearest outline the body doesn't hide; a press on the arrow drags it in any navigation mode, by mouse or finger; each view sizes the arrow and says where it is on screen, `data-arrow`; `data-extrude-profiles`, `-chosen`, `-hover`, `-preview`, `-distance`, `data-arrow-hot`; `data-arrow-drags` counts finished arrow drags, so a test knows a drag's last move has landed). Tested in `test/extrude-pick.test.ts` and `pane.test.ts`. A flat face offered (UX1c, `OfferedProfile.face`) is drawn only under the pointer or chosen (`FILL_OPACITY.faceOffered` 0), and a click on no outline hands over the face under it (`ExtrudePointer` pick's `face`) so a curved one can be refused in words.
- body edges (UX1a): `LineSegments2` strips `EDGE_WIDTH_PX` (1) CSS px wide in `EDGE_COLOUR` (dark grey 0x282b31, highlight.ts; 2 px of near-black was too strong beside Fusion's, Ben 10-26), the material's resolution set from each view's renderer as it draws (split view); pulled towards the camera by a polygon offset (the faces are pushed away), so a finished sketch's line lying on an edge never speckles through it. `fadeBody` tells the edges from the faces by `faceMesh`, since `LineSegments2` is a Mesh. Browser check: `apps/fission/e2e/edge-lines.mjs`.
- Moving the camera by hand has no damping: the camera stops when the button is let go. Finished sketches are depth-tested and drawn before the body, so the body hides what is behind it and a body edge on a sketch line keeps its highlight; the ground grid and axes are a backdrop drawn first with no depth write. `ViewportView.setDrawing(on)` stops and restarts drawing a view (canvas, cube and bar hidden, camera kept; `data-drawing="off"`).
- `viewer.ts` `ViewportView.dispose` (found by the app's soak test): aborts `listening`, the signal on every listener the view puts on its element (which outlives it, split view's pane) and the document (the nav bar's fullscreenchange, `navBar(actions, signal)`), releases what it drew from the shared scene (`release.ts` `releaseShared`: "dispose" dispatched on the scene's geometries, materials, textures and three.js's lookup texture for lit materials, found by drawing a probe into a 1 × 1 target, so no three.js listener keeps the closed renderer; the guide's close does the same), and gives the WebGL context back (`forceContextLoss`); `gpuCounts` and `Viewer.memoryCounts` give the renderer's geometries, textures, programs and the scene's objects for the soak (`fissionTest.gpu()`).
