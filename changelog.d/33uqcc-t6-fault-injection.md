- Testing (T6 (2/2), frontier testing): fault injection in the app. A new journey runs twelve Design steps (Edit feature, a shape change, a delete, drawing, suppress, Extrude, Undo, Redo) once for each kernel call they make, with that call failing (108 runs). Each time nothing may crash, the step must happen whole or not at all, nothing may stay broken once the kernel works again, and every body must be freed. It found five bugs, each fixed with a regression test first. The serious one: deleting a sketch on a face while the kernel failed once moved its extrude down to the ground plane for good (now the delete is refused with the error). The others were in the body cache: a body whose view failed once kept failing until it was rebuilt, and when freeing a body failed, a finished rebuild was thrown away and the body was never freed (now it is tried again later). Each fix was checked by undoing it and seeing the tests catch it.
- Testing (T6 (1/2), frontier testing): fault injection. The kernel's test builds can now make any one step of an operation fail on purpose (`kernel/src/failpoint.rs`; release builds and the app don't have it). A new test runs each operation with its first step failing, then its second, and so on until it completes, the way SQLite tests running out of memory. Every time, the operation must report that exact failure, leave its inputs untouched, and the next call must work exactly as before. Covered: extrudes, extrudes in a turned frame, every body the store builds (a failure must not change the store's bodies, memory count or handles), and every stage of the facts table (seven). Checked on planted operations that swallow a failure, change it, change their input or leave themselves broken (each caught), and on three deliberate kernel mistakes (each caught).
